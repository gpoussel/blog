# Banlist and structural tells

The full reference for words, phrases, and structures that make prose read as
generated. Scan this before finalizing a post. The rule isn't "never, under any
circumstance" so much as "if you wrote this on autopilot, stop and rewrite the
sentence in plain language". When a word here shows up, it's usually a sign the
sentence is filling space instead of saying something.

## 1. Banned vocabulary

**Significance puffers:** pivotal, crucial, vital, key (as adjective), significant,
essential, groundbreaking, remarkable, transformative, profound, testament, enduring,
lasting, paramount, indispensable, invaluable, quintessential.

**Inflated verbs:** underscore, highlight (meaning "emphasize"), showcase, foster, garner,
bolster, delve, embark, leverage, facilitate, utilize, encompass, cultivate, elucidate,
illuminate, navigate (figurative), exemplify, embody, transcend, harness, spearhead,
streamline, galvanize.

**Poetic nouns (used figuratively):** tapestry, landscape, realm, paradigm, ecosystem,
journey, nexus, interplay, mosaic, fabric, cornerstone, beacon, pillar, catalyst, crucible,
linchpin, hallmark, confluence, odyssey, trajectory, underpinning.

**Promotional adjectives:** vibrant, rich, comprehensive, robust, seamless, innovative,
dynamic, cutting-edge, meticulous, intricate, nuanced, nestled, breathtaking, renowned,
bustling, stunning, multifaceted, holistic, overarching, compelling.

**Puffery adverbs:** seamlessly, meticulously, profoundly, intrinsically, fundamentally,
remarkably, notably, crucially, undeniably, inherently, relentlessly, tirelessly, vividly.

**Stiff connectives (use the plain word):** furthermore → also, moreover → also,
consequently → so, accordingly → so, nonetheless → still, nevertheless → still,
additionally → also, thus → so, hence → so.

**Copula avoidance (just use "is"/"has"):** serves as → is, stands as → is, boasts → has,
features (meaning "has") → has, emerged as → became, constitutes → is.

## 2. Banned phrases

**Opener crutches:** "In today's world", "In today's fast-paced world", "In the
ever-evolving landscape of", "In an era of/where", "As we navigate the complexities of".
Start with a concrete fact, a scene, or the actual problem instead.

**Closer crutches:** "In conclusion", "In summary", "Overall", "At the end of the day",
"The bottom line is", "Last but not least", "Without further ado", "In a nutshell".

**Fake authenticity:** "Here's the thing:", "But honestly?", "Here's the truth:", "Let me
be clear:", "But here's where it gets interesting...", "Let me break this down...", "Think
about it this way...". These imitate a candid voice; a real candid voice doesn't announce
itself.

**Filler to cut or rewrite:** "plays a crucial role" → say the action, "when it comes to" →
use a verb, "in order to" → "to", "a wide range of" → name them, "needless to say" → cut,
"it goes without saying" → cut, "more often than not" → "usually", "at this point in time"
→ "now", "in recent years" → give the year, "dive deep into" → just discuss it.

**Passive hedging frames (cut the frame, state the fact):** "It should be noted that",
"It must be emphasized that", "It is important to remember that", "There are several
factors that". Just say the fact.

**Vague attribution:** "Experts argue", "Observers note", "Studies show", "Research
suggests", "It is widely regarded as", "Many believe", "According to some". Name the
specific source, or remove the claim.

## 3. Banned sentence structures

**Negative parallelism:** "It's not just X, it's Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y" /
"Not only X, but also Y". State what the thing *is*, directly.

**False range:** "From X to Y" as a vague figurative spectrum ("from tiny scripts to
sprawling systems"). Only use "from X to Y" for a real, measurable range.

**Participial tack-ons:** ending a sentence with ", highlighting the importance of...",
", underscoring the significance of...", ", reflecting a broader trend toward...". If the
clause adds real information, make it its own sentence. If not, delete it.

**Staccato triplet:** "No meetings. No managers. Just work." Three punchy parallel
fragments in a row is now a recognized generated-text pattern. One short sentence does the
job.

**The rule of three:** listing exactly three items, three adjectives, three examples, by
reflex. Count your lists. If they're all threes, vary them.

**Compulsive restatement:** the last sentence of a paragraph repeating the first in
different words. Delete it; trust the reader.

## 4. Idioms to skip

These are the idioms a non-native author would only use to "sound native". They read as
borrowed, and they're clichés anyway: "at the end of the day", "game-changer", "hit the
ground running", "low-hanging fruit", "move the needle", "circle back", "deep dive", "boil
the ocean", "the elephant in the room", "drink the Kool-Aid", "secret sauce", "bread and
butter", "no-brainer". Plain description always beats a tired idiom.

## 5. Punctuation and rhythm

- **Em dashes:** roughly one per 500 words, max. This blog avoids them by convention; prefer
  commas, colons, parentheses, or a new sentence. (See CLAUDE.md.)
- **Semicolons:** rare in casual prose. Prefer a period or a comma with "and"/"but".
- **Exclamation points:** sparingly. One genuine "I finally got it to work!" is charming;
  three in a paragraph reads as forced enthusiasm.
- **Sentence length:** the single most important lever. Mix very short and very long. Never
  three similar-length sentences in a row.
- **Allow small imperfection:** an occasional fragment, a trailing thought, a casual aside.
  Too-clean punctuation reads as machine-made.

## 6. Replacement strategy

When you hit a banned word, **don't reach for a synonym.** A synonym keeps the same empty
sentence with a different mask. Instead, ask what the sentence is actually trying to say,
and write that in the shortest plain words you've got:

- "use" not "utilize"
- "help" not "facilitate"
- "show" not "demonstrate"
- "start" not "embark on"
- "dig into" not "delve into"
- "end" not "conclude"

And if the honest answer is that the sentence wasn't saying anything, delete it. That's the
most common fix of all.
